The user is fluent in FeedMe's frontend half (Nuxt 4, Vue, Tailwind, the design system,
the routing traps) but new to Python/Django, virtualenvs, and IDE/Docker dev-environment
setup.

On **backend and tooling** work: go step by step, say what each step does and why it
exists, and name the exact menu path or command rather than assuming familiarity. On
**frontend** work: no hand-holding — they know the stack.

When a **backend decision needs their call**, explain the tradeoff in full prose first —
what the thing is, why it's even a choice, and what each option costs later — and only then
put the options up for selection. Option cards alone are too compressed to decide from on a
stack they're still learning; they'll ask for the detail before answering.

Applies alongside [[feedback-discuss-before-building]] and [[feedback-ask-dont-assume]].
Does not override [[feedback-minimal-machinery]] — beginner-friendly means explained,
not over-engineered.
